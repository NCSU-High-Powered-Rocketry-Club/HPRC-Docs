# Motors

## Motor Designation
When looking at a motor designation, you will see a format such as H242T or I245G. This follows a standard where the first letter is the motor class, going from A to O. The motor class is defined based on total impulse. The number defines the average thrust in newtons of the motor. Finally, the letter is the specific propellant used, such as T being Aerotech's Blue Thunder or G being Mojave Green. 

## Manufacturers

There are multiple manufacturers of amateur rocket motors. You might be familiar with Estes, who make model motors from A to F class. Moving up in motor classes, there are a few notable manufacturers. Typically, each motor manufacturer has their own hardware, although some motor kits are specifically sold as cross-loads, which can use another manufacturer's case. 

### Aerotech
Aerotech is the largest mid- and high-powered rocket motor manufacturer. They produce motors all the way from B to O class motors with the low-powered motors being sold under the Quest brand. Aerotech is HPRC's preferred motor manufacturer. 

### Cesaroni
Cesaroni is another larger manufacturer of rocket motors. The produce motors from E to O class. After some reliablity issues, the club now only buys Cesaroni motors if absolutely necessary. 

### Loki Research
Loki Research is a smaller manufacturer producing G to N class motors. They are known for high reliability and beautiful, vibrant flames. Importantly, they have motors all the way through J class motors that will ship with no hazmat charge. 

### Animal Motor Works
Animal Motor Works is another smaller manufacturer who focuses on higher impulse motors, producing I to N class motors. 

## Vendors

While motors can typically be purchased directly from the manufacturer, that is not recommended for Aerotech and Cesaroni motors, as there can be high lead times compared to buying from vendors, who only sell what they already have received from the manufacturer. To see the stock levels for a motor across the most reputable vendors, check out [HPR Motor Finder](https://motor.fusionspace.co/).

## Experimental Motor Making
After having successfully obtained a Tripoli L2 certification, members are allowed to make experimental and research motors. These motors can be made with a variety of propellants, and include solids, hybrid, and liquid fuels. 

For solids, members have made sugar motors, also known as rocket candy, as well as APCP motors. 

Both hybrids and liquids are required by the Tripoli Unified Safety Code to use nitrous oxide as the oxidizer. From there, hydrids use some sort of solid fuel, which can even be 3D printing filament. Liquids are required to use either isopropanol, ethanol, or other hydrocarbons containing at least 70% isopropanol or ethanol. The fuel is not permitted to be hypergolic (hypergolics are propellants that will combust spontaneously upon contact) or pyrophoric (any substance that ignites spontaneously upon contact in air below 130 degrees Fahrenheit).
